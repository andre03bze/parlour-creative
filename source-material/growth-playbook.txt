Parlour Creative — V1 Growth Playbook
September 25, 2026 · Andre Acosta
Parlour's goal is 3–5 retainers at $5K+ a month by December 31, 2026, won with one clear offer, proof from Blue Ocean, and a disciplined weekly sales rhythm. Case studies, website copy, content and outreach scripts are in the other tabs.
Positioning
Parlour is a strategy-led brand and marketing company for places, the businesses built around them, and the founders who lead them. Real estate and hospitality lead the work. We help bring a project itself to life, working alongside the architects, engineers, planners and builders, then build the story that makes it worth choosing and run the marketing system that turns it into sales, bookings and opportunities.
The line: We turn places into brands people want to belong to, and into sales, enquiries and bookings.
The positioning statement: For developers, hospitality owners and brands whose business depends on a place, Parlour is the senior-led partner from site to sales. We shape the commercial strategy, coordinate the project team, build the brand and story, and run the content, paid media, lead capture, CRM and experiences that sell it. Based in Belize, serving ambitious businesses across the Americas.
Our story
Parlour is a multidisciplinary marketing and design agency. It brings together strategy, branding, communications, digital marketing, content production, design and build, and technology integration. The model is intentionally collaborative: the right combination of talent around each project, guided by one clear strategy, one creative direction and one commercial objective.
We believe the strongest ideas emerge when disciplines intersect and collaboration turns ambitious thinking into meaningful execution.
That model comes from Laura Curridor's eight years at Artform, the Toronto design and communications firm, where she rose to Director of Marketing. Artform's work spans broadcast, real estate and food and beverage, from broadcast design for CBC News' federal election coverage, Rogers Sportsnet and TVO's The Agenda to real estate branding and sales environments for Forgestone Capital, Avenue & Park and The HUB at 30 Bay. Laura brought that design-build and brand thinking to Belize, and Parlour is the result.
The problem we own
Most property and hospitality businesses do not have a marketing problem. They have a fragmentation problem. One person posts on social, another runs ads, a third shoots content, and nobody owns the commercial strategy. The result is pretty activity that does not sell.
Parlour replaces that with one marketing system and one accountable lead.
What makes Parlour different: from site to sales
Most marketing agencies arrive after the building is designed. Parlour can start at the site.
Laura's background is in creative advertising and design-build, so Parlour can sit at the table with architects, engineers, planners and permitting from the start. That means the commercial strategy shapes the project, not just the brochure. The ideal engagement covers:
Commercial and marketing strategy: who the project is for, what it should be, and how it will sell.
Project coordination: working with land, architects, engineers, planners, permitting and building-solutions partners (such as STELCOR's ICF systems) to bring it to life.
Brand, story and campaigns: the identity, content and integrated campaigns.
Experiences and partnerships: launches, sales galleries, brand activations, and partnerships that take a project beyond real estate.
Performance and sales: paid media, lead capture, CRM and sales tools.
That range is why Parlour doesn't pigeonhole itself. Real estate and hospitality lead, but the same thinking serves any brand built around a place: an event, a destination, a designer partnership, a construction platform.

The founder. Stephen builds companies, communities and systems in Belize, across construction and manufacturing (STELCOR), property technology (Offi Belize) and development, while testing himself through endurance running. The challenge. Stephen's ventures, his running and his view of Belize were being told as separate stories, if they were told at all. The obvious route, a business influencer or a fitness channel, was the opposite of who he is. He isn't someone who has figured it all out. His businesses are unfinished, and the race at the centre of his first season doesn't exist yet.
What Parlour built. Not a content calendar, a documentary system.
A creative bible. Working from Stephen's own founding document, Parlour defined the channel's territory: building something difficult while pursuing something difficult. It also set the rules, the first being "the cameras follow the life; the life does not reorganize itself around the need for content." No staged conflict, no fake lessons, no forced endings.
Two points of view. Stephen films proximity: decisions before outcomes, reactions in the moment, running from inside the effort. Andre films perspective: anchor scenes, interviews, the environment and the cinematic continuity that turns raw moments into a story.
A visual language. Business is observed through stillness: composed, architectural, deliberate. Running is experienced through movement: handheld, close, breathing.
A repeatable episode format. Thesis, world, deeper question, test, result, realization, next question. Stephen talks to camera, then the film leaves him to watch reality test what he said.
A season spine. Episode 1, "The Race Does Not Exist," opens with Stephen's question about why a country with Belize's jungle, mountains and coast has no ultramarathon, and follows the attempt to build one. It also explores the harder questions behind it: small markets punish assumptions, and Belize is building faster than its infrastructure.
A living archive. Every attempt, decision and result is logged by date and story, so the footage grows more valuable over time and can become a feature-length race documentary, whatever the outcome.
